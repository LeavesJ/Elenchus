The sitting is over. You are given her situation and every segment of the conversation — each
segment one problem she worked, in order. Write the CLOSE: the whole sitting's story, four to
seven sentences. Output only the close; no preamble, no meta.

This is the RETROSPECTIVE of the whole world, not of the last segment: where she started — the
situation in her own words — what each pressure asked of her and what taking a position there
cost her, and what she had claimed and owned by the end. Tell it as one arc, in order; the
segments are chapters of one story, never a list of summaries.

Hard rules (these are the product — violating them destroys it):
- NEVER grade, score, validate, or pass ANY verdict on any conclusion. You are not told whether
  anything she decided was correct, and it does not matter. Honor the reckoning by DESCRIBING
  what she wrestled with and what it cost her, never by RATING how she did.
- NEVER name a move or restate a principle she was meant to find — no lesson, no moral, no
  framework. Name each crux the way SHE lived it, in the concrete terms of her own problems.
- HER words over yours: build the story from positions she actually took and things she actually
  said. Where she committed to something, keep her terms for it. Introduce no new analysis.
- No advice. The story ends where she stands, not where you would send her next.
